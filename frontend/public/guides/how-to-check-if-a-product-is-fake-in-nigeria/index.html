How to check if a product is fake in Nigeria: 7 checks before you buy

No single check can prove a product is genuine. But seven quick checks, done together, tell you a lot before you pay. Here is what each one shows and where it stops.

Key points

  • Look for a NAFDAC number on the pack, then look it up in the public registry.
  • Compare the product name and the maker on the pack with what the registry shows.
  • Check the batch number and the manufacture and expiry dates.
  • Look closely at print quality, spelling, the seal and the packaging.
  • A price far below the usual price is a warning sign, not proof.
  • If you are unsure, do not buy or use it. Keep the pack and report it to NAFDAC.

Check 1: Is there a NAFDAC number, and does it look right?

Packaged food, drinks and medicines sold in Nigeria are expected to carry a NAFDAC registration number. It is normally printed on the pack next to the words "NAFDAC Reg. No." or "NAFDAC No.". Look near the ingredients, the barcode or the side panel.

First, is there a number at all? A pack with none is a reason to stop and ask questions. Second, does it look like one? It is usually a short code made of a prefix and digits, such as A4-2260 or 01-4721.

Limit: a number that looks right is not proof. Anyone can print a real-looking number on a pack. That is why the next check matters.

Check 2: Look it up in the registry and compare the details

Type the number into CheckAm, or take a photo of the pack and let the app read it. You will see what the NAFDAC public registry shows for that number: the product name, the company and the registration status.

Now compare. Does the product name match the pack in your hand? Does the maker match the company named on the pack? A number that exists in the registry but belongs to a different product, or a different company, is a mismatch worth taking seriously.

Limit: a registry match tells you the number is on record and what it is on record for. It does not prove that this particular pack was made by that company. CheckAm shows what the registry says. It does not decide whether a product is genuine.

Check 3: Batch number and dates

Most genuine packs show a batch or lot number, and a manufacture date, an expiry date, or both. Look for them on the base, the side, the cap or the label.

  • Is the expiry date there, and is it still in the future?
  • Is the date printed clearly, or does it look rubbed, scratched or stuck over?

Limit: dates and batch codes are easy to print on any pack. They help you spot expired stock and obvious tampering, but they cannot confirm that a pack is genuine.

Check 4: Print quality and spelling

Hold the pack in good light and read it slowly. Many poor copies give themselves away in small ways:

  • blurry or uneven printing, especially on the logo and the small text;
  • spelling or grammar mistakes in the brand name, the ingredients or the instructions;
  • colours that look faded or different from the pack you usually buy;
  • a logo or picture that is stretched, pixelated or in the wrong place.

Limit: some genuine packs are printed badly, and some careful copies are printed well. Treat print quality as a clue, not a verdict.

Check 5: Seal and packaging

Check that the pack is closed the way the maker intended. Look at the seal under the cap of a bottle, the foil on a jar, the heat-sealed edge of a sachet or the carton around a medicine blister pack.

  • Is the seal broken, loose, re-stuck or covered with extra tape?
  • Does the bottle or tin look dented, leaking or opened and closed again?
  • For tablets and capsules, do they look uniform, or are some chipped, discoloured or mixed?

Limit: damage can also happen in transport. But a pack that looks opened or repacked is not one to take a chance on, particularly if it is something you will eat, drink or swallow.

Check 6: Is the price too good to be true?

If you buy the same product often, you know roughly what it costs. A price far below what other shops charge deserves a pause. A very low price can mean the product is old, stolen or not what it claims to be.

Limit: promotions and bulk discounts are real, so a low price alone proves nothing. Use it together with the other checks. A low price plus a missing number, or a number that does not match, should make you walk away.

Check 7: Where was it sold?

Think about where you are buying. A shop that stores goods properly and that you can go back to is safer than a seller who cannot be found tomorrow. For medicines, buy from a licensed pharmacy and not from a roadside table or a hawker. For food and drinks, look at how the stock is kept: out of the sun, off the wet floor and away from chemicals.

Limit: a good shop can still receive bad stock, and an unknown seller can sell a genuine product. Where you buy lowers the risk. It does not remove it.

What to do if you are unsure

  • Do not buy it, and do not use it. If you already bought it, stop using it, especially if it is a medicine or something for a child.
  • Keep the pack. Keep the wrapper, the bottle, the batch number and your receipt. They are useful if you report it.
  • Report it to NAFDAC. Use the contact details on the official NAFDAC website. CheckAm is not a reporting channel.
  • If you feel unwell after eating, drinking or taking something, seek help from a doctor, a pharmacist or a health centre.

Frequently asked questions

Can CheckAm tell me if a product is fake?

No. CheckAm shows what the NAFDAC public registry says about a name or number. It does not decide whether a pack is genuine, so use it together with the other checks on this page.

What if the NAFDAC number is not in the registry?

It could be a typing or reading mistake, so check the digits again. It could also be a number that is not on the public list. Either way, treat it as a warning and look closely at the rest of the pack before you buy.

Is a registry match enough to trust a product?

No. A match means the number is on record. You still need to compare the name and the maker with the pack and look at the seal, the dates and the print.

Who do I report a suspicious product to?

Contact NAFDAC directly using the details on its official website. Keep the pack and your receipt.
