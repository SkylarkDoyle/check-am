How to check if food and drinks are NAFDAC registered

Packaged food and bottled drinks should be registered before they are sold. Here is how to check one in a few minutes, and how to read the result.

Key points

  • According to NAFDAC public statements, packaged food and drinks need registration before sale.
  • This includes bottled local drinks such as zobo and tigernut drink.
  • Find the NAFDAC number on the pack, then look it up by number or by name.
  • Compare the name and the maker in the registry with the pack.
  • A registry match is a helpful check, not a guarantee. Look at the seal and the dates too.

Which food and drinks should be registered?

NAFDAC has said publicly that packaged food and drinks need to be registered before they are sold. This covers the kinds of products you see in supermarkets, kiosks and markets: biscuits, noodles, seasonings, cooking oil, bottled water, soft drinks, juices and many others.

It also includes local drinks that are packed in bottles or sachets for sale, such as zobo and tigernut drink. Many small producers do this well and have a registered product. If a drink is sold in a labelled pack, it is fair to expect a NAFDAC number on it.

The rules have detail that we do not cover here. For the official position on what needs registration, see the NAFDAC website.

Step 1: Find the number on the pack

Look for "NAFDAC Reg. No." or "NAFDAC No." and the code after it. It is usually a prefix and digits, for example A4-2260 or 01-4721. Check the label, the back of the pack, the side panel and the area near the ingredients and the barcode.

Write the number down or take a clear photo. Then note the brand name and the name of the company on the pack, because you will compare them with the result.

Step 2: Look it up

Open CheckAm and do one of these:

  • Type the NAFDAC number. This is usually the most exact way.
  • Type the product name. Useful when the number is hard to read, though a name can match more than one product.
  • Take a photo of the pack. Drag the box over the number and the app reads it on your phone. If a character is wrong, fix it before you check.

CheckAm keeps the registry list on your phone, so you can look something up at the market even with no data.

Step 3: Read the result and compare

The result shows what the NAFDAC public registry has on record for that number or name. Compare it with the pack in your hand:

  • Does the product name agree with the label?
  • Is the company the same as the maker named on the pack?
  • Does the registration status look current?
  • If you searched by name, is the entry the same product, with the same size or variant?

If something does not agree, do not assume the worst, but do not ignore it either. It may be a spelling difference, an older registration or a different variant. It may also be a number that belongs to a different product.

Step 4: Look at the rest of the pack

A registry check does not look at the pack. You do that part. Check the expiry date, the seal, the cap and the print quality. For drinks, look at the colour and clarity of the liquid, and check that the bottle is not leaking or swollen. For more detail, read how to check if a product is fake in Nigeria.

When the product is not found

Do not stop at the first search. Try these:

  • Read the number again and correct any wrong digit.
  • Search by brand name, then by the name of the maker.
  • Try a shorter form of the name, without the size or flavour.

If it still cannot be found, it could be a product that is not on the public list, or a number that is not real. CheckAm cannot tell you which. Ask the seller to show you the registration, and think about buying a product you can verify instead. If you are worried about a product, report it to NAFDAC through its official website.

Buying from small producers and markets

Many good products come from small producers. A missing number does not mean that a producer is dishonest, but it does mean you cannot check the product against the registry. If you choose to buy it, you carry that risk yourself. Pay extra attention to how the product is stored, whether the pack is sealed and whether the producer's name and address are on the label.

Frequently asked questions

Do bottled drinks like zobo and tigernut drink need NAFDAC registration?

Based on NAFDAC public statements, drinks sold in packs need registration before sale, and that includes local drinks. For the official rules, see the NAFDAC website.

Can I check food by name instead of number?

Yes. You can type the product name in CheckAm. A name can match more than one product, so compare the maker and the details with the pack.

Does a registry match mean the food is safe to eat?

No. It means the number is on record. It does not tell you the pack is genuine, in date or well stored, so look at the pack too.

Does it work without internet?

Yes. CheckAm keeps the registry list on your phone, so searching works offline.
