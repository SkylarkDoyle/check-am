What does a NAFDAC registration number look like?

It is a short code printed on the pack. This guide describes what you can safely say about it, and is open about what we cannot say.

Key points

  • It is printed on the pack next to the words NAFDAC Reg. No. or NAFDAC No.
  • It is made of a prefix and digits, for example A4-2260 or 01-4721.
  • The format can differ between product types, and between older and newer products.
  • We do not explain what individual letters or digits mean, because we cannot verify it.
  • Looking the number up in the registry is more useful than judging it by its shape.

Where is the number printed?

Look for the words "NAFDAC Reg. No." or "NAFDAC No." on the pack. The number follows those words. On many products it sits close to the ingredients list, the barcode, the batch details or the side panel. On a small sachet it may be on the back or along an edge. On a bottle, check the label, and sometimes the back label.

If you cannot find it, turn the pack over and read all sides slowly. Some products print it in small type.

What kind of code is it?

In general, a NAFDAC registration number is a short code. It is made of a prefix, which can be letters, digits or a mix, followed by digits. Many packs show a dash between the prefix and the rest of the number.

Two examples of the shape you may see are:

  • A4-2260, a letter and a digit, a dash, then four digits;
  • 01-4721, two digits, a dash, then four digits.

These are examples of the shape only. They are here to show you what to look for on a pack, not to tell you what a given product is.

Do all numbers look the same?

No. The format is not the same for every product. Numbers can look different between product types, such as foods, drinks and medicines, and between older and newer products. You may also see small differences in spacing, dashes or capital letters from one pack to another.

So do not reject a pack only because its number does not match an example you have seen. Look the number up instead.

What we cannot tell you

You may read online that each prefix letter or each group of digits stands for something, such as a product type or a year. We do not explain what individual prefix letters or digits mean, because we cannot verify it, and we would rather leave it out than guess. If you want an official explanation, ask NAFDAC through the contact details on its official website.

For the same reason, CheckAm does not try to judge a number by its shape. A number that "looks right" can be copied onto any pack, and a real number can be printed in an unusual style.

A better test than looking at the shape

The most useful thing you can do with a number is look it up. Here is how:

  1. Read the number carefully from the pack. Zero and the letter O, and the number 1 and the letter I, are easy to mix up.
  2. Type it into CheckAm, or take a photo of the pack and let the app read it. If a character is misread, you can correct it before you check.
  3. Read the result: the product name, the company and the registration status shown in the NAFDAC public registry.
  4. Compare the result with the pack in your hand. The name and the maker should agree.

If the number is not found, check the digits again before you decide anything. A single wrong character will give no result.

What a result does and does not mean

If the registry shows the number, you know it is on record and you can see what it is on record for. That helps you notice a mismatch, such as a number that belongs to a different product.

It does not prove that the pack in your hand is genuine. CheckAm shows what the registry says. It does not decide whether a product is genuine. For the other things to look at, such as the seal, the dates and the print, read our guide on how to check if a product is fake in Nigeria.

Numbers that are missing, damaged or unclear

  • No number at all. Ask the seller why. A packaged product without a number is worth thinking twice about.
  • A smudged or scratched number. Try the product name in the search instead, and compare the maker and the address on the pack.
  • A number that is hard to read in a photo. Move into better light, hold the phone steady and drag the box over the number.
